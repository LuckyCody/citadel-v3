Models Citadel's non-interactive envelope (KEM+KDF+AEAD, v1/v2 suites, fresh
per-envelope nonce, atomic replay claim) against a Dolev-Yao attacker who fully
controls the network (intercept / modify / inject / replay / attempt downgrade).

RESULTS:
  (1) Secrecy            RESULT not attacker(secretPt) is true.              PROVED
  (2) No-downgrade+bind  RESULT ReceiverAccepts ==> SenderSeals is true.     PROVED
  (3) Replay (injective) RESULT inj ... cannot be proved.                    INCONCLUSIVE

Interpretation:
- (1) An attacker without the recipient secret key never learns the plaintext.
- (2) The receiver only ever outputs the honest plaintext under the exact
      suite/context/aad the sender used. The sender always seals v2 and the
      receiver tries BOTH v1 and v2, so a working downgrade (accepting the
      honest plaintext under v1) would break this — it does not. This is the
      symbolic form of Citadel's no-silent-downgrade + AAD/context binding.
- (3) ProVerif finds NO replay attack (not "false") but cannot complete the
      injective proof — a known ProVerif limitation reasoning about a stateful
      atomic claim. Modeling the claim's atomicity with a lock token removed the
      spurious table-race counterexample; the residual is tool incompleteness,
      not a Citadel weakness. Replay ATOMICITY is separately assured by the real
      ReplayStore::claim (atomic test-and-set) and exhaustively verified in
      Tier 6 (Loom).

CAVEATS (honest): symbolic/Dolev-Yao model — primitives are perfect, so this
proves PROTOCOL-level properties, not the computational crypto (Tier 9 combiner
analysis + Tier 1 vectors cover that). The AEAD/KDF binding is encoded as a
property of `open`, reflecting the design that Tiers 1b/9 check at the
implementation level. No sender-authentication is claimed (the envelope is
encryption, not signcryption; ML-DSA signing is a separate path).
